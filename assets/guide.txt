@topic Getting Started | What Azeroth Survivors is and how a run works
# What is Azeroth Survivors?
Azeroth Survivors is a survivors-style game set in classic World of Warcraft, made by Aethus. You pick a hero, enter a zone and stay alive while an ever-growing horde closes in. Your spells cast on their own: you move, dodge and choose how your hero grows.

# How a run works
- Pick a class and a zone on the main screen, then press Enter (or the big Enter button).
- Move to begin. Monsters pour in from every side and your spells fire at them automatically.
- Slain enemies drop experience and gold. Every level-up deals you cards: pick one to learn a new spell, upgrade one you have, or take a bonus.
- Bosses arrive on a fixed clock. The zone's timeline on the main screen shows who comes and when.
- Beat the final boss to win the zone. That opens the next zone for the class you played.

# What you keep after a run
Even a lost run counts. Talent experience, boss gear, achievements and newly opened zones stay with you. Gear and looks are kept per class, and achievements and rewards count for your whole account.

@topic Controls | Keys, mouse and gamepad
# Moving
- W, A, S, D: run forward, turn left, run back, turn right (arrow keys work too).
- Q and E: strafe left and right.
- Hold both mouse buttons to run where the camera points.
- Space: jump. Num Lock: auto-run.

# During a run
- Your spells and your racial ability cast on their own. Your job is to move, dodge and pick upgrades.
- 1, 2, 3: pick a card when you level up. R: reroll the cards.
- X: skip a level-up or cache hand when its Skip button is available. Change this under Key Bindings > Azeroth Survivors > Skip. Outside cards, X still makes your hero sit or stand.
- Y: open your stored level-ups (when Store Level-Ups is on).
- Escape: the menu (Back, Options, End Run).

# Names and chat
Hero and lobby names, lobby chat and in-run chat support Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Cyrillic and accented Latin letters. Chinese, Japanese and Korean input methods show your composition in every text box in both the game and browser. Enter confirms a character without sending the line or starting the run. Names and chat display across language settings, including writing from other languages.

# On the main screen
- Enter starts the run in the selected zone.
- Every key can be changed in Options > Key Bindings.

# Camera distance
You can set how far the camera sits from your hero, both from the main menu and in the middle of a run.

# Gamepad
Gamepads are supported. On menus, the D-pad or left stick moves between buttons, A presses and B goes back. Y opens stored level-ups.

# Useful options
- Controls > Store Level-Ups: level-ups no longer pause the fight. They are saved for later and a Level Up! button shows how many are waiting.
- Graphics > Level-Up Rays: turn off the spinning rays behind the level-up cards.
- Controls > Open Caches by Clicking: walking over a cache no longer buys it; right-click it instead.
- Interface: turn parts of the run's screen on or off: the run clock and next-boss line, the boss and guardian bars, the kills and gold box, your spells and jewelry, the banners in the middle of the screen, the boss loot notice and the damage meter.

# Nameplates and local time
Options > Combat > Nameplate Layout changes at once, even during a run. Stacking spreads enemy nameplates apart for readability; Overlapping keeps each plate directly above its enemy.
A clock beneath the minimap shows your device's local time: 12-hour time in English and 24-hour time in other languages. It moves and hides with the minimap and follows HUD Scale. This is separate from the run timer.

# The damage meter
The meter window has three views: Damage Done (every spell, pet, talent and item, with its share of the run), Healing Done (the health each source gave you, overhealing left out) and Boss Damage (the last boss fight only). Lines in blue are a talent's or amulet's own damage. Its Reset button only clears the window: the run's numbers are kept.

@topic Your First Run | A step-by-step start for new heroes
# Before you start
- Pick a class on the main screen. Hover the spells in its Spell Pool to see what it can learn.
- Your hero's race is chosen in Armory > Character. Each race has its own racial bonuses.
- Elwynn Forest is the first zone. Every other zone opens by beating the one before it, separately for each class.

# In the run
- Keep moving. Standing still lets the horde surround you.
- Walk over gold coins and experience. They fly to you once you are close enough.
- Read each level-up card before you pick. A new spell fills one of your six spell slots; an upgrade makes one of your spells stronger.
- Watch the clock and the "next boss" timer under it. Bosses hit hard and mark the ground before big attacks: step out of the markers.
- Caches cost gold and offer jewelry. Guardians wait in lairs and give a reward when slain.

# After the run
- If you fall, choose Death Recap on the end screen to see the killing blow and review the last hits, damage by source and avoidable boss damage. In co-op, each player gets a recap of their own fall; close it with its button or Escape.
- Open Talents to spend the talent points you earned.
- Check Drop Tables to see which boss drops which piece of your class's gear.
- New achievements give points to spend on perks in Rewards.

@page Warrior | Huge health, cleaving swings, fury on every kill
"Unstoppable in the thick of it. Huge health, cleaving swings, and every kill feeds the fury."
- Health: 230
- Starts with: Cleave
- Plays best: in melee range, in the middle of the horde.

# Spell pool
- Cleave (starting spell): hits up to 3 enemies in front of you. Bosses and elites are targeted first.
- Whirlwind: hits every enemy close around you.
- Thunder Clap: hits every enemy around you and slows them.
- Mortal Strike: a heavy blow on the nearest enemy.
- Execute: a strike that deals double damage to enemies below 30% health.
- Demoralizing Shout: hits everything around you; enemies hit deal less damage for a few seconds.
- Battle Shout: a big damage boost for a few seconds, used once enemies are near.
- Bloodthirst: strikes the nearest enemy and heals you for half the damage dealt.
- Rend: makes the healthiest enemies nearby bleed over time.
- Shield Slam: strikes up to 2 enemies and stuns them briefly.
- Bloodrage: your hits build rage; at full rage you erupt and damage every enemy around you.

# Talent trees
- Arms: Deep Wounds, Sweeping Strikes, Mortal Wounds
- Fury: Rampage, Flurry, Bladestorm
- Protection: Shockwave, Fortified, Titan

@page Paladin | Holy ground, righteous hammers, self-healing
"Holy ground and righteous hammers. Heals himself, shrugs off death once in a while."
- Health: 245
- Starts with: Consecration
- Plays best: close to the horde, with heals and a shield to fall back on.

# Spell pool
- Consecration (starting spell): blesses the ground where you stand; every enemy on it takes Holy damage over time.
- Exorcism: a strong strike on the nearest enemy.
- Hammer of Wrath: strikes the nearest enemy from range.
- Holy Shock: fires missiles at random enemies; each hit heals you.
- Holy Wrath: hits every enemy around you.
- Judgement: a very heavy strike on the nearest enemy.
- Hammer of Justice: strikes the 2 nearest enemies and stuns them.
- Holy Light: a big heal when your health falls low.
- Divine Shield: makes you immune to all damage for a few seconds when your health falls low.
- Holy Shield: for a while, enemies that hit you in melee take damage.
- Redemption: slain enemies sometimes burst in holy light, damaging everything near them.

# Talent trees
- Holy: Divine Favor, Daybreak, Light's Grace
- Protection: Hallowed Ground, Ardent Defender, Hammer of the Righteous
- Retribution: Seal of Command, The Art of War, Divine Storm

@topic Races | The eight races and their racial abilities
Choose your race, gender and looks in Armory > Character. Each race has a racial ability that casts on its own, plus passive bonuses. Not every race is open to every class at first: hover a race portrait to see where to open it.

# Any race, any class
On the armory's Rewards tab, the Races section opens a race to every class for 100 achievement points, with its racial ability and passives. A Tauren Paladin, a Gnome Druid, an Undead Shaman: it's up to you. A race you buy is yours for good (a perk reset keeps it). A druid of a Horde race takes the Tauren's cat and bear forms, an Alliance one the Night Elf's.

# Alliance
- Human: Perception (every 30 sec, more damage for a short time), The Human Spirit (+20% experience), Diplomacy (+20% gold).
- Dwarf: Stoneform (every 30 sec, a shield worth 45% of your max health), Gun Specialization (+6% damage), Treasure Finding (+30% pickup range).
- Night Elf: Shadowmeld (near death, 3 sec of immunity, long cooldown), Quickness (+8% movement speed), Nature Resistance (+5% damage reduction).
- Gnome: Escape Artist (every 20 sec, roots nearby enemies), Expansive Mind (-8% cooldowns), Engineering Specialization (+20% luck).

# Horde
- Orc: Blood Fury (every 40 sec, +35% damage for a while), Hardiness (+10% max health).
- Undead: Will of the Forsaken (near death, 4 sec of immunity), Cannibalize (every kill heals you a little), Shadow Resistance (+4% damage reduction).
- Tauren: War Stomp (every 15 sec, stuns nearby enemies), Endurance (+15% max health).
- Troll: Berserking (every 35 sec, faster cooldowns for 10 sec), Regeneration (+1.5 health per second), Throwing Specialization (+3% critical strike).

@topic Zones & Bosses | All twelve zones, how to open them and who you face
Every zone ends with a final boss. Beat it to open the next zone, for that class. The bosses before it arrive on a fixed clock, and each boss can drop one piece of your class's gear.

# Short zones (final boss at 16:00)
- Elwynn Forest (common gear): Hogger 3:00, Mother Fang 6:00, Rhahk'Zor 9:00, Mr. Smite 12:00, Edwin VanCleef 16:00. Open from the start.
- Duskwood (common gear): Naraxis 3:00, Mor'Ladim 6:00, Stitches 9:00, Lupos 12:00, Morbent Fel 16:00. Beat Edwin VanCleef to open.
- Warsong Gulch (uncommon gear): Doomguard Commander, Mannoroth's Herald, Varimathras, Lord Hel'nurath, Lord Kazzak at 16:00. Beat Morbent Fel to open.
- Un'Goro Crater (uncommon gear): Ravasaur Matriarch 3:00, Uhk'loc 6:00, Lar'korwi 9:00, Tyrant Devilsaur 12:00, King Mosh 16:00. Beat Lord Kazzak to open.

# Boss fights
Every boss has its own moves: charges, blinks behind you, eggs that hatch, totems and more. Before a big attack the ground is marked. Step out of the marker: getting caught hurts twice as much. The horde also grows tougher as the minutes pass, so the last bosses are the real test.
- Several bosses can be up at once if you have not finished the last one when the next arrives.
- Below 30% health a boss enrages and uses its abilities more often: save your cooldowns and your distance for that part.
- Leaving a ground circle counts as soon as you are outside it on your screen.
- Highlord Mograine's Mark stops following you soon enough to escape on Heroic, and deals damage in line with the other Horsemen's Marks. Scarlet Commander Mograine's second Mark also gives you time to escape.
- Creature sizes affect melee reach: larger bosses reach farther, while smaller elites reach less far.
- Bosses aim a little ahead of where you are running: blasts, breaths, charges, beams, volleys and rains lead you, and more so on each Heroic level. Change direction instead of running in a straight line.

@topic Level-Ups & Cards | How your hero grows during a run
# Level cap
Your hero can reach level 120 during a run.

# Picking cards
Each level-up deals you three cards. Pick one with the mouse or with 1, 2 or 3. A card can teach you a new spell from your class's spell pool, upgrade a spell you already have, or give you a bonus. You have six spell slots, so choose which spells you build around. Hover the spells shown under the cards to read their full tooltips.

# Rerolls
- Press R (or the Reroll button) for a fresh hand.
- Every run gives you three free rerolls. After that, rerolls cost gold, and the price rises with each one.
- Perks from the Rewards tab add more free rerolls, let you banish a card for the rest of the run, or skip a hand for gold.

# Skipping cards
When the Skip button is available, press X to skip the hand, including cache cards. The button shows the current binding; change it under Key Bindings > Azeroth Survivors > Skip. This key acts on cards only.

# Stored level-ups
With Options > Controls > Store Level-Ups switched on, a level-up no longer pauses the fight. Your levels wait until you open them with the Level Up! button above your action bar, or with Y. They are offered one after another until none are left. Switching the setting off offers every stored level at once.

# The clock
The run's clock stops while the cards are on screen and while the game is paused, so taking your time over a pick costs you nothing.

# Luck
Luck gives you better odds of rarer upgrades when you level up. You can see your luck in the Run Stats next to the character window during a run.

# A moment of safety
After you pick a card or an item you are immune to damage for one second.

@topic Pickups, Caches & Guardians | Gold, experience, jewelry and the monsters worth hunting
# Gold and experience
Slain enemies drop gold coins and experience. Walk close and they fly to you; more pickup range pulls them from further away. Gold buys caches and rerolls.

# Magnets
A floating blue rune in a pillar of blue light. Walk over it and every coin and every bit of experience on the whole field flies to you. A run has two or three. Bosses drop them, and they never disappear, so you can save one for when the field is covered in gold.

# Caches
Caches are chests on the field that cost gold to open, and the price rises as you buy more (the run's screen shows the next price). A cache offers jewelry cards: rings, trinkets and amulets. Elites and guardians offer jewelry too. Each class also has legendary amulets of its own, one for every spell. The Codex in the Armory shows every piece the game has offered you so far. In co-op, every hero buys their own caches. If an amulet would replace the one you wear, the game asks you to confirm and shows both amulets side by side with their stats. Co-op guests can see the stats on rings, trinkets and amulets too.

# Guardians
Guardians are big monsters that wait in their lairs. A red flag on the minimap points to the nearest lair (or, when every lair is empty, the one that fills soonest). Slay a guardian for a reward pick. Each one you slay makes the later ones tougher, and an empty lair fills again after a few minutes. They also have their own moves, so treat them like small bosses.
- A lair is asleep (its guardian waits), awake (its guardian is fighting) or empty.
- A guardian wakes when you come near. It fights where it stands, but walks back home if you lead it too far from its lair.

# Elites, runes and soulstones
- Elites are stronger enemies in the horde, worth a jewelry pick. Many spells aim at bosses and elites first.
- Battleground zones have runes on the field, such as Speed and Berserking, that give a short buff.
- A Major Soulstone holds an extra life (two at most). After it brings you back, you are protected from damage for 2 seconds.

@topic Gear & the Armory | Boss loot, your collection and how your hero looks
# Boss gear
Each boss drops one piece of gear for your class, on its own roll. The zone decides the rarity: common, uncommon, rare, epic, PvP and legendary. The rarer the gear, the lower the chance: from 60% in Elwynn Forest down to 20% in the last two zones on normal, and higher on each Heroic level. Gear is kept per class.
- Bad luck protection: each kill of a boss that misses its piece makes that piece likelier to drop the next time.
- A piece you already own pays marks instead.

# Each class's gear
Every piece of every class, with its bonuses, the bosses that drop it and each set's 4-piece and 8-piece bonuses: Warrior Gear, Paladin Gear, Hunter Gear, Rogue Gear, Priest Gear, Shaman Gear, Mage Gear, Warlock Gear and Druid Gear.

# Slots
Head, Shoulders, Chest, Hands, Wrist, Waist, Legs, Feet and Main Hand. The final boss of a zone drops the main-hand weapon.

# The Armory tabs
- Character: race, gender, looks, and the gear you wear. A soft oval glow sits directly under your hero's feet.
- Collection: every piece for your class by rarity. Click a piece to see its bonuses and the bosses that drop it. A piece can be worn, or only shown as a look.
- Drop Tables: every zone's bosses, their arrival times, and the piece and chance each one drops.
- Collection also shows the pieces still to find in your zones, with their boss and drop chance. Once you have them all, it shows each set's star progress instead: stars per piece, its 4-piece and 8-piece bonuses and how many stars are open.
- Codex: every ring, trinket and amulet the game has offered you.
- Talents, Achievements and Rewards: see their own topics.

# Marks
Every boss you kill pays your class marks: more in harder zones and on higher Heroic levels, and half on normal. Bosses that return in endless mode pay more each time. A piece you already own pays marks instead when a boss drops it.
- Marks are paid the moment the boss dies, so they count even if you fall later in the run.
- Clearing a zone or a Heroic level pays nothing extra: every mark comes from the bosses and the duplicates they drop.
- Marks belong to the class that earned them: they do not carry over from your main.
- Spend them in the armory on stars (see Stars). The goal is to be fully geared and fully upgraded before you start the next Heroic level; if you are good, start it earlier.

# Stars
Spend marks in the armory to raise a piece, weapons included, from 0 to 5 stars. Each star adds to the piece's own bonuses.
- A set's stars open as your class beats Heroic levels in that set's zones: Heroic I opens the 1st star, Heroic II the 2nd, Heroic III the 3rd (and with it the 8-piece bonuses, which need 3 stars), Heroic IV the 4th and Heroic V the 5th.
- 5 stars make the legendary sets 60% stronger (the patch notes call them tier 3: the sets from Gates of Ahn'Qiraj and Naxxramas).
- Lower sets gain more from each star, and their 5th star is a big one: it gives their armor the stats the legendary sets have and they lack (echo on every set; common pieces also get the legendary sets' extra stat), and lifts them close to a 5-star legendary set: 90% for common, 92% uncommon, 94% rare, 96% epic and 98% PvP, so your favourite set stays viable at the end and keeps the spells you like.
- Lower sets are cheaper to upgrade. The last star costs what a legendary set's does: 30 marks.
- Every other class that has a set's armor at a star rank makes that rank 5% cheaper for yours, up to 40%.
- Not known yet: how much each single star adds, for each set and quality, and what each star costs. The Gear Planner does not count stars.

# Set bonuses
- 4-piece: four armor pieces of one set at 1 star or more give the set's bonus. It is the same for every class: common +25% gold and +30% pickup range, uncommon 1.5% of your max health per second after 3 seconds unhurt, rare +1 target to every ability, epic 6% chance for an ability to strike twice, PvP +12% damage reduction with 5 or more enemies close, legendary +30% critical damage.
- Sets can be mixed: four pieces of one set and four of another give both 4-piece bonuses.
- 8-piece: all eight armor pieces of one set at 3 stars or more change how one of your class's spells plays, a different spell for each set. For example, Fireball splits, Aimed Shot pierces, and Consecration, Blizzard and Volley move with you. At 5 stars on all eight the bonus is empowered: half again as strong.
- Most PvP sets are built around crowd control: their 8-piece bonus makes your stun, root, slow or fear spell (Hammer of Justice, Concussive Shot, Kidney Shot, Psychic Scream, Frost Nova, Death Coil, Shred) hit more enemies, harder and more often, and all your damage higher against enemies you stun, root, slow or fear. The warrior's is built around Mortal Strike and the shaman's around Earth Shock instead.
- An armor piece's tooltip (character pane, C) shows its set: the set's name with how many pieces you wear, its pieces, and its 4-piece and 8-piece bonuses with the stars they need. Each class's sets and their bonuses are listed under its gear in the Items topic.

# Looks
Appearances, tabards and weapon glows only change how your hero looks: see the Transmog topic.

@topic Talents | Talent points, trees and specs
# Earning points
Every run pays talent experience to the class you played: for time survived, kills, bosses and a win, and more in harder zones and on higher Heroic levels.

# Spending points
- Each class has three talent trees.
- Point at a talent to read it. Click to spend a point; right-click to take one back.
- A new row opens for every 5 points spent in a tree.
- At 10, 20 and 30 points in a tree you reach its milestones, which change how your spells play.
- You can have three specs (saved setups) per class, and respecs are free.

Every class page in the Classes topic shows its three full trees, and which talents change each of its spells.

@topic Achievements & Rewards | Account-wide goals and what they unlock
# Achievements
Achievements are earned by every hero of every class, for your whole account. They come in groups: Zones, Classes, Collection, Feats, Lifetime and Heroic. Win zones, collect gear, survive long into a run, or chase special feats like big kill counts and winning without gear.

# Rewards
Achievements give points to spend on the Rewards tab. Rewards are account-wide too.
- Perks: Second Thoughts (more free rerolls), Skip (pass on a hand and take gold instead), Veteran's Lessons (more talent experience for a class behind your best), Banish (remove a card for the rest of the run), Opening Hand (your first level-up deals four cards) and Keen Eye (bosses drop their piece more often). Each perk is unlocked by an achievement.
- Companions: a critter that follows every hero and fetches coins and experience for you. Instincts make it prefer gold, experience or battleground runes, and you can unlock a second companion and many looks.
- Tabards and appearances: looks only. Battleground tabards come with their zone's achievement.
- Races: 100 points opens a race to every class, for good (see Races).
- Co-op achievements: earned by everyone in the party. A boss's killing blow counts for every player still in the run, whoever lands it.
You can take back points spent on perks and spend them again; the panel shows how many resets you have left.

@topic Heroic & Endless | Heroic I to V for better rewards, and what comes after a win
# Heroic I to V
Pick a Heroic level with the Difficulty button beside Play. It shows each level's enemy health and damage, its rewards and its twists. Each level makes the horde and the bosses tougher and adds two twists, such as elite packs, relentless bosses, stronger guardians, a leaner purse, horde affixes, the old tower and gaze mechanics, harsher failed mechanics, halved healing, and a final boss that brings a friend.
- In return, boss drop chances, talent experience and marks rise with the Heroic level, and Heroic levels open your gear's stars (see Gear & the Armory).
- Heroic I of a zone opens once any of your classes beats the zone. Beating Heroic N there opens the next level for every class.
- A class can play a zone's Heroic once it has beaten that zone itself.
- Against the Odds, and the co-op feats that asked for maxed Run Options before, now ask for Heroic III or harder.

# Horde affixes
From Heroic III the horde has an affix that changes every week; Heroic V has two at once.
- Bolstering: each death makes the enemies within 12 yards 20% tougher and stronger, stacking up to triple.
- Sanguine: slain elites leave wide pools that heal the horde 10% a second (the horde only, not bosses or lair guardians).
- Explosive: an orb appears every 15 seconds and bursts for 40% of your health unless you destroy it. Area spells pass through it: it takes five direct hits from aimed spells and weapon swings.

# Endless mode
After you beat a zone's final boss you can keep going in endless mode. A random boss from the zone arrives every five minutes and the horde keeps growing stronger. Each boss that returns pays more marks than the time before. See how long you can last; some achievements ask for it.

@topic Co-op | Playing with friends
# Joining and hosting
Press Co-op on the main screen. Host and guests must use compatible game versions: 0.128.0 requires everyone to update.
A co-op run needs at least two players: a host alone sees "Waiting for players" until someone joins.
- Lobbies: public games of your version. Each shows its zone, its difficulty (Normal, or the Heroic level in red), the host's class, the party's classes and the open seats. Click one to join (some need a password).
- Filters: by zone and by difficulty. "Can join" hides every lobby whose zone or Heroic level your class has not opened yet, and a lobby you cannot join says which of the two you are missing.
- Host: name your lobby, choose who can find it (public, or private by code), set the number of players (up to 5) and an optional password. Hosting shows the run you will host (hero, zone, difficulty), with a Change difficulty button.
- Join by code: type a friend's lobby code.
- Party chat: while you wait in the lobby, a chat sits beside the party (it closes when the run begins). Messages are up to 160 characters; press Enter or click Send. The Co-op button shows how many came in while the window was closed. Chat stays steady as messages arrive, and scrolling up keeps your place while new messages come in.
- Each guest's ping shows the true round trip to the host: green when quick, gold when fair, red when slow. Before 0.127.0, it included up to 50 ms of the game's own waiting; older guests still show that old measurement. The party window shows each player's country next to their ping.
You play the hero on your own picker. The host chooses the zone and the Heroic level, and everyone in the party sees them change at once. You can only join zones (and Heroic levels) you have opened with the class you bring: a hero who has not is marked in red and stays behind when the host enters.

# Inspecting a party member
Click any player's row in the co-op party window to inspect their hero. The Armory shows their character, gear, stars, appearance and combined stats with active set bonuses. Point at a gear piece to see its stats at its current stars, which set it belongs to and how many pieces they wear.
- The Talents tab shows the player's three trees as they spent their points. Point at a talent to read what it does.
- The Achievements tab shows what their account has earned, grouped by category with the date earned.
- The inspection window updates automatically as the player changes race, appearance, gear, stars or talents in the lobby.
# How co-op plays
- Experience and gold are shared; caches are each hero's own to buy.
- Every extra hero makes the horde bigger (each brings 35% fewer extra enemies than before 0.125.0), but the party also finds more chests. In a party every enemy (mobs, elites, guardians and bosses) has 20% less health and hits 20% softer; playing alone is unchanged.
- When you pick a card, only you pause while the others fight on.
- A fallen hero is down, not out: stand next to them for 6 seconds to bring them back. Each extra ally reviving the same friend speeds it up by 20% (two allies 1.2x, three 1.4x, four 1.6x). The run ends when every hero is down.
- Your heals, Battle Shout and Power Word: Shield also help friends within 30 yards.
- Every hero rolls on a slain boss's gear for their own class and earns their own marks and clears, and a guardian slain by anyone rewards everyone.
- A Heroic clear counts the moment the final boss falls, even if someone leaves before every level-up card is picked.
- Everything you win goes to your own save.

@topic Addons | Add-ons for the game's interface, and where they go
Azeroth Survivors runs on the World of Warcraft 1.12 client, so it uses 1.12 addons. They can change the interface, add windows and read the run as it goes: the clock, the bosses, your spells, the damage meter and more.

# Installing
- With the launcher: open Addons, then Download More. INSTALL puts an addon in its place, and the list keeps it up to date. Every addon is optional and starts off.
- By hand (Windows): put the addon's folder in benilla-config\AddOns, next to the game.
- In the browser version: Options > AddOns, then install a .zip of the addon's folder.
- An addon loads the next time you enter the world.

# Old addons
An addon made for a different game version only loads with "Load out of date AddOns" switched on. Addons built for this game have the launcher's AZS ADDONS label; the WOW 1.12 ADDONS are classic addons converted to work here.

# Replacing the game's HUD
An addon can hide a part of the game's own screen (the clock, the boss bars, the damage meter and so on) to draw its own. Those are the same "Show ..." settings as Options > Interface, so you can always switch them back there.

# Addon frames over cards
Addon authors can use AZS_SetOnTopOfScreens to keep their frames above the level-up and new-item cards.

@topic Tips for New Players | Small things that make a big difference
- Never stop moving. Walk in wide circles so the horde follows you in a line instead of surrounding you.
- Learn your starting spell's range. Melee classes want to be close; casters and hunters want room.
- Area spells (Consecration, Blizzard, Volley, Rain of Fire and friends) win the early minutes. Strong single-target spells win boss fights.
- Take a heal or a shield when your class offers one. Staying alive beats a few percent more damage.
- Save a magnet for later: it never disappears, and it is worth more when the field is full of gold.
- Buy caches early. Jewelry snowballs over the run.
- Hunt guardians when you feel strong. Their rewards are worth the detour.
- Lost a run? You still earned talent experience. Spend it in Talents and go again.
- Try every class. Talent experience and gear are kept per class, and achievements count for your whole account.
